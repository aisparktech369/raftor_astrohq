*These are compiled notes about a traditional source. The exact passage, edition and translation have not yet been checked against the original, so they are not given as scripture. They describe what the tradition is said to hold; they do not predict anything about you.*

## Ayurveda on where dreams come from

The notes say Ayurveda (Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita) explains dreams as products of mental impressions and the condition of the body: daily experiences, desires, fears, physical imbalance and impressions held in the mind.

## The seven kinds of dream

| Kind | What it is said to be |
|---|---|
| Dream from experience | Events seen or heard during the day |
| Dream from desire | Unfulfilled wishes |
| Dream from imagination | The creative activity of the mind |
| Dream from mental impressions | Memories stored in the mind |
| Dream from physical imbalance | Bodily conditions |
| Dream from anxiety | Emotional disturbance |
| Prophetic dream | A dream believed to carry a symbolic or predictive meaning |

## The three doshas and dream content

| Dosha | Images linked with it |
|---|---|
| Vata dreams | Flying, falling, running, movement through the sky |
| Pitta dreams | Fire, lightning, intense heat, bright colours |
| Kapha dreams | Water, rivers, lakes, rain |

These links describe bodily and mental states in the tradition's own terms. They are not a diagnosis of anyone.

## Garuda Purana: images read as favourable

The notes say the Garuda Purana describes dream symbols as signs that may reflect future events or moral conditions.

| Dream | How the tradition reads it |
|---|---|
| Seeing cows | Prosperity and abundance |
| Seeing elephants | Power and success |
| Seeing clear water | Purity and peace |
| Seeing gold or jewels | Wealth and fortune |
| Climbing mountains | Achievement and success |
| Seeing fruit-bearing trees | Growth and prosperity |

## Garuda Purana: images read as unfavourable

| Dream | How the tradition reads it |
|---|---|
| Seeing broken buildings | Instability or loss |
| Falling from a height | Loss of status |
| Being attacked by animals | Danger or conflict |
| Seeing dark or muddy water | Confusion or emotional disturbance |
| Being chased | Fear or unresolved issues |

## Remedies the tradition describes

The notes list bathing, prayer, reciting sacred hymns, charity and worship of deities as spiritual remedies for disturbing dreams, believed in the tradition to reduce their effects. They are practices described by the tradition, not instructions and not a promise.

## How the tradition combines factors

Indian dream interpretation is described as weighing the symbols, the feeling in the dream, the physical and mental condition of the dreamer, the timing and the spiritual context together, and as being read with reflection rather than certainty.
