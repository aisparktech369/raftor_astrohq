*This is classical numerology, a historical doctrine. It is not a fact or a prediction.*

Classical numerology prefers the name number to harmonize with the birth number. It says a person may add, remove, or change a letter or name to create a correspondence. It also compares the name and birth number with a house number and dates, describing place as a larger “octave,” a house as more intimate, and a date/week-day as more immediate.

It says to keep compounds and roots visible. The book’s examples support showing each name word’s compound/root and then any combined-name result, but the aggregation is not explained uniformly in every example.

**A note:** Any “harmony,” “clash” or luck is classical numerology's claim. Changing a legal name is not advised because of a number, and doing so is not shown to improve anything. House and place-name arithmetic uses the traditional name table.
