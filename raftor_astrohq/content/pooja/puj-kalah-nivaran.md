## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Deity | Shiva and Parvati and their family (Shiv Parivar) |
| What | A pooja with a prayer for harmony at home |
| When | Mondays or Purnima |
| Who | The family, or a priest for them |
| Time taken | About an hour |
| Shelf | Samasya samadhan, for a family dispute |
| What it seeks | Blessings (ashirwad) for this aim. The result rests with the divine, and no one can promise it |

## Story and meaning

| Story | What is told |
|---|---|
| The Shiv Parivar | Shiva, Parvati, Ganesha and Kartikeya are held up as a united family in the tradition |
| Harmony | The prayer asks for patience and clear speech in the home |
| The meaning | The pooja does not settle a dispute. Talking does |

## When to perform

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Day | Monday or Purnima |
| Time | Evening, with the family together |
| Alongside | Honest talk, and a counsellor or mediator if needed |

## Items (samagri)

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| An image of the Shiv Parivar | The seat of the pooja |
| Bilva leaves, white flowers | Offered |
| Milk, fruit, sweets | The food offered |
| Incense, lamp | The usual upachara |

## Steps

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Sankalp | The family's names and the aim are stated |
| Ganesh pooja | Ganesha is honoured first |
| Shiv Parivar pooja | The family is offered the upachara |
| A shared prayer | The family chants together |
| Aarti and prasad | The lamp is waved and prasad is shared |

## Mantras

| Mantra | Use |
|---|---|
| Om Namah Shivaya | For Shiva |
| Om Gauri Shankaraya Namah | For Shiva and Parvati |

## Questions people ask

<details><summary>Will it end the quarrel?</summary><p>No. It is a prayer for peace. A quarrel ends when people talk and listen.</p></details>

<details><summary>Who should be present?</summary><p>As many of the family as will join, if they are willing.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does it guarantee a result?</summary><p>No one can promise a result. A pooja is an act of seeking blessings. The priest and AstroHQ are only the means (nimit) of offering it, and what is received rests with the divine.</p></details>
