## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Deity | Vishnu, as Satyanarayan |
| What | A pooja with the katha, the story, read in five chapters |
| When | Purnima (full moon) days, and at a new home, a wedding, a birth or a success |
| Who | The family, with a priest or an elder reading the katha |
| Time taken | About two to three hours |
| Shelf | Sanskar and occasions |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Story and meaning

| Story | What is told |
|---|---|
| The source | The katha is told in the Reva Khanda of the Skanda Purana |
| The five chapters | They tell of those who kept the vow and were blessed, and of those who forgot it |
| The meaning | Satya means truth. The pooja holds that truth and keeping one's word are what the vow honours |
| The prasad | A sweet of semolina or wheat flour, ghee, sugar and banana, with tulsi leaves |

## When to perform

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Day | Purnima is the most common. Thursday is also favoured, for Vishnu |
| Occasion | Many families do it before a new beginning or in thanks |
| Muhurat | Your town's panchang gives the time |

## Items (samagri)

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| Picture or idol of Satyanarayan | The seat of the pooja |
| Kalash, banana plant or leaves | The mandap is often made with banana plants |
| Panchamrit | Milk, curd, honey, ghee and sugar for the bath of the deity |
| Tulsi leaves, yellow flowers | Offered to Vishnu |
| Prasad ingredients | Flour or semolina, ghee, sugar, banana |
| Incense, lamp, fruit, betel | The usual upachara |

## Steps

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Sankalp | The name, family line and the reason are stated |
| Ganesh pooja | Ganesha is honoured first |
| Kalash and Vishnu | The kalash is set up and Satyanarayan is invited |
| Panchamrit bath | The image is bathed and decorated |
| Katha | The five chapters are read, with a pause and aarti after each, in some families |
| Aarti | The lamp is waved and the aarti is sung |
| Prasad | The prasad is shared with everyone present |

## Questions people ask

<details><summary>Is it done only on Purnima?</summary><p>Purnima is the most common day, and families also do it on other auspicious days.</p></details>

<details><summary>Can it be done at home?</summary><p>Yes. It is among the most common home poojas, with a priest or an elder reading the katha.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does it guarantee a result?</summary><p>No. It is an act of devotion and not a guaranteed outcome.</p></details>
