## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| What it is | Sun or Moon with Rahu or Ketu, or the ninth house afflicted, by the common reading |
| How it is read | The Sun, the ninth house and Rahu and Ketu in the chart |
| Who advises it | [An astrologer](/consult), from the birth chart |
| Time taken | One to three hours, more for large rites |
| Shelf | Graha shanti, doshas and yogas |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Story and meaning

| Story | What is told |
|---|---|
| The ancestors | The Garuda Purana and the Dharmashastras teach that the living remember their ancestors with water (tarpan), food (shraddh) and gifts |
| A dosha is not a verdict | Astrologers differ on how much weight a dosha carries, and many charts carry rules that cancel it (parihar). Ask for the reading, not a fear |
| The pooja | A pooja or havan asks for peace from the influence. It is a prayer and not a cure |

## When to perform

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Day | Pitru Paksha, the fortnight of the ancestors, and Amavasya |
| Place | Gaya, Haridwar, Prayag or a river bank |
| Who | The sons and family of the line |

## Items (samagri)

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| Black sesame, water, kusha grass | For the tarpan |
| Rice balls (pinda), barley | Offered |
| Food, clothes for the needy | Given in the ancestors' name |
| Flowers, incense, lamp | The usual upachara |

## Steps

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Sankalp | The family line and the ancestors are named |
| Ganesh pooja | Ganesha is honoured first |
| Tarpan | Water with sesame and kusha is offered to the ancestors |
| Pinda daan | Rice balls are offered |
| Brahmin and charity | A priest is fed, and a gift is made |
| Prayer | A prayer for the ancestors' peace is made |

## Mantras

| Mantra | Use |
|---|---|
| Om Pitrubhyo Namah | A common mantra of remembrance |
| Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya | Chanted for the ancestors' peace |

## Questions people ask

<details><summary>Is it a curse?</summary><p>It is a reading. The rites are those of remembrance and gratitude.</p></details>

<details><summary>Can it be done in my name?</summary><p>The rites are best done in person, with the family, at the place. A priest can do a part in your name.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does it guarantee a result?</summary><p>No. It is an act of devotion and not a guaranteed outcome.</p></details>
