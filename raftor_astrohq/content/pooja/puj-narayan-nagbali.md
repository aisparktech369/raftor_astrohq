## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| What it is | A reading of unsettled ancestors, or of harm done to a serpent |
| How it is read | From the chart and the family history, by [an astrologer](/consult) |
| Who advises it | [An astrologer](/consult), from the birth chart |
| Time taken | One to three hours, more for large rites |
| Shelf | Graha shanti, doshas and yogas |
| What it seeks | Blessings (ashirwad) for this aim. The result rests with the divine, and no one can promise it |

## Story and meaning

| Story | What is told |
|---|---|
| Two rites | Narayan bali is for the peace of ancestors who died by accident or untimely. Nag bali is for the sin of killing a snake |
| A dosha is not a verdict | Astrologers differ on how much weight a dosha carries, and many charts carry rules that cancel it (parihar). Ask for the reading, not a fear |
| The pooja | A pooja or havan asks for peace from the influence. It is a prayer and not a cure |

## When to perform

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Place | Trimbakeshwar near Nashik is the traditional place |
| Duration | Three days |
| Who | A married couple does it together, as a rule in the tradition |

## Items (samagri)

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| Wheat or rice flour figures | Made for the rite |
| Black sesame, kusha, water | For the tarpan |
| Havan items | The fire offerings |
| Gifts and food | Given to priests and the needy |

## Steps

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Sankalp | The names, family line and the purpose are stated |
| Narayan bali | The rite for the ancestors |
| Nag bali | The rite for the serpent |
| Havan | Offerings into the fire |
| Daan | Gifts and food are given |
| Closing | A prayer for peace |

## Mantras

| Mantra | Use |
|---|---|
| Om Namo Narayanaya | A common mantra for Vishnu |
| Om Pitrubhyo Namah | A mantra of remembrance |
