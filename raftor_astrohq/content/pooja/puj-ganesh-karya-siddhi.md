## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Deity | Ganesha |
| What | A pooja with Ganesh mantras and the Atharvashirsha |
| When | Wednesdays, Chaturthi or Sankashti Chaturthi |
| Who | The person, or a priest on their behalf |
| Time taken | One to two hours |
| Shelf | Kamna purti, for a career |
| What it seeks | Blessings (ashirwad) for this aim. The result rests with the divine, and no one can promise it |

## Story and meaning

| Story | What is told |
|---|---|
| Ganesha | The Puranas tell that Parvati made Ganesha, and Shiva gave him an elephant's head |
| Vighnaharta | Ganesha is Vighnaharta, the remover of obstacles, and is worshipped first |
| The Atharvashirsha | The Ganapati Atharvashirsha is the Upanishad that praises him |

## When to perform

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Day | Wednesday, Chaturthi or Sankashti Chaturthi (the fourth day after the full moon) |
| Time | Morning after a bath |
| The prayer | For a clear path to a task, with effort of one's own |

## Items (samagri)

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| An image of Ganesha | The seat of the pooja |
| Durva grass | Twenty-one blades are traditional |
| Red flowers, sindoor | Offered |
| Modak or laddu, incense, lamp | The usual upachara |

## Steps

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Sankalp | The name, family line and the task are stated |
| Ganesh pooja | Ganesha is invited and offered the upachara |
| Atharvashirsha | The Upanishad is recited |
| Japa | Om Gam Ganapataye Namah is chanted |
| Aarti and prasad | The lamp is waved and prasad is shared |

## Mantras

| Mantra | Use |
|---|---|
| Om Gam Ganapataye Namah | The common beej mantra of Ganesha |
| Vakratunda Mahakaya | A well-known Ganesh shloka |

<details><summary>Why durva?</summary><p>In the tradition durva is the grass Ganesha is offered.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does it guarantee a result?</summary><p>No one can promise a result. A pooja is an act of seeking blessings. The priest and AstroHQ are only the means (nimit) of offering it, and what is received rests with the divine.</p></details>
