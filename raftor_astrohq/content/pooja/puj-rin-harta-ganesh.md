## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Deity | Ganesha, as Rin-harta, the remover of debt |
| What | A pooja with Ganesh mantras and the Rinaharta stotra |
| When | Wednesdays and Chaturthi, in common practice |
| Who | The person concerned, or a priest on their behalf |
| Shelf | Samasya samadhan, for debt |
| What it seeks | Blessings (ashirwad) for this aim. The result rests with the divine, and no one can promise it |

## Story and meaning

| Story | What is told |
|---|---|
| Ganesha | The Puranas tell that Parvati made Ganesha, and Shiva gave him an elephant's head. He is worshipped first, at every beginning |
| Remover of obstacles | Vighnaharta, the remover of obstacles, is his best-known name |
| Rin | Rin means debt, in money and in duty. The prayer asks for steadiness to meet it |
| The stotra | A hymn called the Rinaharta Ganesh stotra is recited in this pooja |

## When to perform

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Day | Wednesday, the day of Ganesha, and Chaturthi |
| Time | Morning, after a bath |
| Alongside | A plan to repay, and honest advice on money. A pooja is not a substitute |

## Items (samagri)

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| Image of Ganesha | The seat of the pooja |
| Durva grass | Traditionally twenty-one blades, offered to Ganesha |
| Red flowers, sindoor | Offered |
| Modak or laddu | The food offered |
| Lamp, incense | The usual upachara |

## Steps

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Sankalp | The name, family line and purpose are stated |
| Ganesh pooja | Ganesha is invited and offered the upachara |
| Mantra | Om Gam Ganapataye Namah, chanted on a mala |
| Stotra | The Rinaharta Ganesh stotra is recited |
| Aarti and prasad | The lamp is waved and prasad is shared |

## Questions people ask

<details><summary>Will it clear my debt?</summary><p>No. It is an act of devotion. Debt is cleared by a plan to repay, and by advice from someone who knows your finances.</p></details>

<details><summary>Why Wednesday?</summary><p>Wednesday is Ganesha's day in common practice.</p></details>

<details><summary>Can it be done in my name?</summary><p>Yes. The priest states your name and family line. Ask how it will be shared with you.</p></details>
