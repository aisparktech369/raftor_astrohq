## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Deity | Agni, with Ganesha, the nine planets, and Shiva as Mrityunjaya |
| What | A havan for long life and good health |
| When | On the birthday, by the date or by the birth star |
| Who | The person, with a priest |
| Time taken | One to two hours |
| Shelf | Sanskar and occasions |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Story and meaning

| Story | What is told |
|---|---|
| The prayer | Ayushya means long life. The havan asks for good years, not for any one thing |
| Mrityunjaya | The Rigveda mantra to Shiva as Mrityunjaya is chanted for health and long life |
| The birth star | Many families keep the birthday by the star (nakshatra), not only the date |

## When to perform

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Day | The birthday by the English date or by the birth star, as the family keeps it |
| Muhurat | The priest takes the best hour that day |
| Place | The home, or a temple |

## Items (samagri)

| Item | Use |
|---|---|
| Havan kund, wood, ghee, samagri | For the fire |
| Kalash, flowers, rice | The seat and the offerings |
| Sesame and herbs | Offered in some families for health |
| Sweets, fruit, incense, lamp | The usual upachara |

## Steps

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Sankalp | The name, family line, age and purpose are stated |
| Ganesh pooja | Ganesha is honoured first |
| Navagraha pooja | The planets are honoured |
| Havan | Offerings with the Mrityunjaya and other mantras |
| Blessings | Elders bless the person |
| Prasad | Prasad is shared |

## Mantras

| Mantra | Use |
|---|---|
| Maha Mrityunjaya mantra | From the Rigveda, for health and long life |
| Om Namah Shivaya | A common short mantra |

## Questions people ask

<details><summary>Is it only for the elderly?</summary><p>No. Families do it for children and adults on a birthday.</p></details>

<details><summary>Is it a health measure?</summary><p>No. It is a prayer. Care for health comes from a doctor, a good routine and rest.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does it guarantee a result?</summary><p>No. It is an act of devotion and not a guaranteed outcome.</p></details>
