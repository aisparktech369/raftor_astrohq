## Four ways a pooja can be done

| Way | Who performs | Your name and gotra | You see it | Cost | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| In person, at home or a temple | You, or a priest beside you | Stated by the priest | Yes, you are there | Highest | Rites that need the family, such as Griha pravesh and weddings |
| Virtual, live, in your name | A priest, at a temple or home | Stated in the sankalp | Yes, by live video | Medium | Distance, NRIs, a busy day |
| Virtual, recorded, in your name | A priest, earlier | Stated in the sankalp | A video, after | Medium | When timing does not suit you |
| Group (samuhik) | Several priests, for many devotees | Your name is taken, then joined into one common sankalp | Often live, or a recording | Lowest | Festivals, a shared prayer |

## What the tradition says

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| The sponsor and the priests | In the Vedic yajna the sponsor (yajamana) pays for and owns the rite, and priests (ritvij) perform it for them. The priest standing for the sponsor is the oldest form of the idea |
| The sankalp | The resolve names the person, the family line and the aim. The rite is held to be for the one named |
| Worship in the mind | Mental worship (manasa puja) is a recognised path. The Shiva Manasa Puja, attributed to Adi Shankara, offers a whole pooja in the mind |
| Rites for many | Large rites such as Mahayajnas and temple poojas have long been done for a community's welfare |
| Status of the claim | These are statements of religious tradition. Teachers differ, and some prefer that you are present. They are not scientific findings |

## How far it counts

| Question | An honest answer |
|---|---|
| Does a virtual pooja count? | Many traditions hold that it does when the sankalp names you and the priest is trained. Some teachers prefer presence. No one can measure it |
| Is it the same as being there? | Not quite. Presence is your own act. Joining live, fasting, and chanting along are ways to take part |
| Does a group pooja give the same result as a personal one? | Sellers say yes. No text cited here says so. It is a valid act of worship and a share in a common prayer. A personal sankalp is for a personal matter |
| Is a result guaranteed? | No. Neither kind can promise one |
| Is there evidence? | There is no scientific evidence of results from any pooja. It is an act of faith and devotion |

## Which to choose

| Your situation | A sensible choice |
|---|---|
| You live far away | Virtual, live, in your name |
| A festival and a small budget | A group pooja |
| A personal matter, such as a planet's period or a dosha | A personal pooja in your name, with your details |
| A rite the family must attend | In person |
| Ancestors (Pitra) | In person, at the place the rite is done |
| You cannot be there at the time | Virtual, recorded. Say a prayer at the hour |

## How to check a virtual pooja is real

| Check | Why |
|---|---|
| A live option | A live pooja cannot be a re-used video |
| Your name and gotra spoken | The sankalp is the heart of it. Ask to hear it |
| A named priest and place | You should know who performed it and where |
| The date and time on the video | A recording should be of your pooja, on your date |
| What the sankalp covers | The names, the aim and the number of mantras |
| No promised outcomes | Be wary of any pooja that guarantees a result |
| A clear refund and rescheduling rule | In case it cannot be done as booked |

## If you do not know your gotra

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Ask first | Elders in the family, or the family priest, may know it |
| If it cannot be found | Common practice is to take the gotra Kashyapa, as the sage from whom all beings are said to descend. Ask your priest |

## Questions people ask

<details><summary>Does a pooja done in my name really reach me?</summary><p>The tradition holds that a sankalp naming you makes the rite yours. Whether and how it works cannot be measured, and a result cannot be promised.</p></details>

<details><summary>Is a group pooja less effective than a personal one?</summary><p>No text cited here says so, and none says otherwise. A group pooja is a shared prayer at a lower cost. A personal matter is usually given a personal sankalp.</p></details>

<details><summary>Should I watch it live?</summary><p>It helps. Live viewing lets you hear your name and gotra and take part by chanting along.</p></details>

<details><summary>Can I do both?</summary><p>Yes. Many families do a personal pooja for their own matter and join a group pooja at a festival.</p></details>
