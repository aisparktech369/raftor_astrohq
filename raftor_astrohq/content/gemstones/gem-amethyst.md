## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Amethyst, Jamunia |
| Mineral | Quartz (silicon dioxide). Purple from iron and natural radiation |
| Hardness | 7 on the Mohs scale |
| Traditional association | Saturn (Shani), in practice as a lower-cost alternative to blue sapphire |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Where it comes from

| Origin | What to know |
|---|---|
| Brazil, Uruguay | The largest sources, with deep purple from Uruguay |
| Zambia | Deep purple stones |
| Bolivia, Madagascar | Further sources |
| Russia (Urals) | A historic source |

## Natural, treated, or made

| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| Natural, untreated | Natural colour, cut and polished |
| Heated | Heat turns amethyst yellow to orange, sold as citrine. Pale stones may be heated or irradiated |
| Synthetic | Lab-grown amethyst is common and hard to tell by eye |
| Look-alikes | Purple glass and dyed quartz |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the tradition says | Jyotish practice uses amethyst as a lower-cost alternative to blue sapphire for Saturn. It is a different stone |
| Texts | The Garuda Purana and Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita describe qualities and faults of gems |
| Status of the claim | The planetary link is a matter of religious tradition. It is not a scientific property of the stone or a promise of any result |

## What to ask for

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| An independent laboratory report | States natural or synthetic and any heating |
| Colour zoning | Natural amethyst often shows uneven colour bands |
| Weight in carats | Compare price per carat |

## Questions people ask

<details><summary>Is amethyst the same as Neelam?</summary><p>No. Neelam is blue sapphire. Amethyst is purple quartz and costs much less.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does heat change amethyst?</summary><p>Yes. Heat can turn it yellow, which is sold as citrine.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does it guarantee the traditional benefit people talk about?</summary><p>No. Traditional associations are matters of belief, not guaranteed outcomes.</p></details>
