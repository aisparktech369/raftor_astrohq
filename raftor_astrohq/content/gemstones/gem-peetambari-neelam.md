## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Peetambari Neelam, a trade name |
| Mineral | In the market it usually describes sapphire (corundum) showing yellow and blue. Confirm on the report |
| Hardness | 9 on the Mohs scale if it is sapphire |
| Traditional association | Sellers often link it with Saturn and Jupiter together. This is a market claim |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Where it comes from

| Origin | What to know |
|---|---|
| Sri Lanka | The best-known source of parti-coloured sapphire |
| Madagascar, Thailand, Australia | Other sources of bicolour and parti-colour sapphire |
| Not set by the name | The name does not tell you the origin. Only a report can |

## Natural, treated, or made

| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| Natural, heated | Common in sapphire. Must be stated |
| Diffused | Colour added at the surface. Must be stated |
| Synthetic | Lab-grown stones exist in many colours |
| Other materials | Some stones sold under this name are other minerals or glass |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the tradition says | This name is a market term. Jyotish texts speak of the blue sapphire for Saturn and the yellow sapphire for Jupiter, not of a single combined stone |
| Texts | The Garuda Purana and Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita describe qualities and faults of gems |
| Status of the claim | The planetary link is a matter of religious tradition. It is not a scientific property of the stone or a promise of any result |

## What to ask for

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| An independent laboratory report | The species and colour in the report's own words |
| Natural or synthetic, and treatment | Stated plainly |
| What the seller means by the name | Ask in writing, as the term has no fixed meaning |
| Weight in carats | Compare price per carat, not per ratti |
