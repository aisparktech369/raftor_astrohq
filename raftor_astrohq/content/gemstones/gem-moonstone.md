## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Moonstone, Chandrakant |
| Mineral | Feldspar (adularia, an orthoclase). The glow is called adularescence |
| Hardness | 6 to 6.5 on the Mohs scale, with cleavage |
| Traditional association | The Moon (Chandra), in practice as a lower-cost alternative to pearl |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Where it comes from

| Origin | What to know |
|---|---|
| Sri Lanka | The best-known source of blue-sheen moonstone |
| India | Sources with varied quality |
| Myanmar, Madagascar, Tanzania | Other sources |
| Not set by the name | "Rainbow moonstone" is usually a white labradorite from several places |

## Natural, treated, or made

| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| Natural, untreated | Most moonstone is only cut and polished |
| Treated | Some stones are dyed or coated. Must be stated |
| Look-alikes | Glass, "opalite" (glass) and plastic are not moonstone |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the tradition says | Jyotish practice uses moonstone as a lower-cost alternative to pearl for the Moon. It is a different stone |
| Texts | The Garuda Purana and Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita describe qualities and faults of gems |
| Status of the claim | The planetary link is a matter of religious tradition. It is not a scientific property of the stone or a promise of any result |

## What to ask for

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| An independent laboratory report | States the species |
| Species in words | Moonstone, or rainbow moonstone (labradorite) |
| Care | Soft with cleavage. Avoid knocks and ultrasonic cleaners |

## Questions people ask

<details><summary>Is rainbow moonstone real moonstone?</summary><p>It is a different feldspar, labradorite, sold under the name. Ask for the species in the report.</p></details>

<details><summary>Is moonstone the same as pearl?</summary><p>No. It is a mineral, and pearl is organic.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does it guarantee the traditional benefit people talk about?</summary><p>No. Traditional associations are matters of belief, not guaranteed outcomes.</p></details>
