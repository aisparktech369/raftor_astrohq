## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Citrine, Sunela (a trade name) |
| Mineral | Quartz (silicon dioxide). Yellow from iron |
| Hardness | 7 on the Mohs scale |
| Traditional association | Jupiter (Guru), in practice as a lower-cost alternative to yellow sapphire |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Where it comes from

| Origin | What to know |
|---|---|
| Brazil | The main source, from Rio Grande do Sul |
| Zambia, Madagascar | Further sources |
| Bolivia | Source of ametrine (amethyst and citrine in one stone) |
| Not set by the name | Deep orange "Madeira" colour is usually heated amethyst |

## Natural, treated, or made

| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| Natural, untreated | Pale yellow. Rare |
| Heated amethyst | Most citrine on the market. Heat gives yellow to orange. Normal if stated |
| Synthetic | Lab-grown citrine exists |
| Look-alikes | Yellow glass and yellow topaz |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the tradition says | Jyotish practice uses citrine as a lower-cost alternative to yellow sapphire for Jupiter. It is a different stone |
| Texts | The Garuda Purana and Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita describe qualities and faults of gems |
| Status of the claim | The planetary link is a matter of religious tradition. It is not a scientific property of the stone or a promise of any result |

## What to ask for

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| An independent laboratory report | States natural or synthetic and any heating |
| Species in words | Quartz (citrine), not sapphire or topaz |
| What "Sunela" means | Ask in writing. It is a trade name |

## Questions people ask

<details><summary>Is citrine the same as Pukhraj?</summary><p>No. Pukhraj is yellow sapphire. Citrine is quartz and costs much less.</p></details>

<details><summary>Is natural citrine common?</summary><p>No. Most citrine is heated amethyst.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does it guarantee the traditional benefit people talk about?</summary><p>No. Traditional associations are matters of belief, not guaranteed outcomes.</p></details>
