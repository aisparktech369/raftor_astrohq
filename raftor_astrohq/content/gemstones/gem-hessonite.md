## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Hessonite, Gomed, gomedha |
| Mineral | Garnet, grossular variety (calcium aluminium silicate) |
| Hardness | 7 to 7.5 on the Mohs scale |
| Traditional association | Rahu |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Where it comes from

| Origin | What to know |
|---|---|
| Sri Lanka | The best-known source of fine honey-coloured stones |
| India | Smaller sources |
| Brazil, Tanzania, Madagascar | Modern sources |
| Canada (Quebec) | A source of hessonite and other grossular garnet |

## Natural, treated, or made

| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| Natural, untreated | Most hessonite is sold untreated |
| Dyed or coated | Colour added to pale stones. Must be stated |
| Look-alikes | Orange glass, citrine and other garnets are different materials |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the tradition says | Jyotish practice links hessonite with Rahu, and it is one of the nine gems of the Navaratna |
| Texts | The Garuda Purana and Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita describe qualities and faults of gems |
| Status of the claim | The planetary link is a matter of religious tradition. It is not a scientific property of the stone or a promise of any result |

## What to ask for

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| An independent laboratory report | States the species and that it is natural |
| The swirled inside | A treacle-like swirl is typical of natural hessonite. A perfectly clean, deeply coloured stone should be checked |
| Treatment in words | None, or dyed or coated |
| Weight in carats | Compare price per carat, not per ratti |
