## What it is

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is a gemstone? | A mineral (or, for pearl and coral, an organic material) that is cut or polished for wear |
| Why is the market confusing? | The same stone has many trade names, and treated, synthetic and imitation stones are sold beside natural ones |
| Is a traditional benefit guaranteed? | No. Planetary associations are traditional beliefs, not tested properties |

## Where gemstones come from

| Stone | Well-known sources | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Ruby (Manikya) | Myanmar, Mozambique, Thailand, Sri Lanka, Madagascar, Tanzania | Myanmar is the historic benchmark. Mozambique is the large modern source |
| Blue sapphire (Neelam) | Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Madagascar, Thailand, Australia, Kashmir (historic) | Kashmir mines gave very little and mostly long ago |
| Yellow sapphire (Pukhraj) | Sri Lanka, Thailand, Australia, Madagascar | Same mineral as ruby and blue sapphire |
| Emerald (Panna) | Colombia, Zambia, Brazil, Afghanistan, Zimbabwe | Colombia is the historic benchmark |
| Cat's eye (Lehsunia) | Sri Lanka, Brazil, India | A chrysoberyl with a sharp white line |
| Pearl (Moti) | Cultured: Japan, China, Australia, Indonesia, French Polynesia, India | Natural pearls are rare and historic |

## Why the origin matters

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Price | The same quality can cost very different amounts from different origins, because the market pays for the name |
| Quality | An origin does not guarantee quality. Every mine gives fine and poor stones |
| How it is known | A gem laboratory states origin as an expert opinion from the stone's inclusions and chemistry. A seller's word is not evidence |
| Trade names | "Ceylon" means Sri Lanka and "Burma" means Myanmar. A name on a tag is a claim, not a report |

## Natural, treated, or made

| Kind | What it means | Disclosed? |
|---|---|---|
| Natural, untreated | Formed in the earth, only cut and polished | Highest price. Rare in the market |
| Natural, heated | Heated to improve colour or clarity | Very common in ruby and sapphire. Acceptable if stated |
| Natural, filled or diffused | Oil, resin, glass or other material in cracks, or colour added at the surface | Must be stated. Affects price and care |
| Synthetic (lab-grown) | Same chemistry as the natural stone, grown in a laboratory | Not natural. Must be called synthetic or lab-grown |
| Imitation | A different material (glass, dyed quartz) that only looks similar | Not the stone it is sold as |

## Names that cause confusion

| Name you may see | What it is | Often confused with |
|---|---|---|
| Neelam | Blue sapphire | Blue topaz, iolite, glass |
| Pukhraj | Yellow sapphire | Yellow topaz, citrine |
| Sunela | A name used for citrine or yellow topaz | Yellow sapphire. It is a different, cheaper mineral |
| Manikya | Ruby | Red spinel, garnet, glass |
| Panna | Emerald | Green beryl, green glass, dyed quartz |
| Gomed | Hessonite garnet | Other brown or orange stones |
| Hakik | Agate | Other banded stones |

## What the papers should say

| Field | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Laboratory | An independent laboratory, not the seller's own |
| Species and variety | For example corundum, variety ruby |
| Natural or synthetic | Stated plainly |
| Treatment | None, heated, or filled, stated plainly |
| Origin | If given, it is the laboratory's opinion |
| Weight | In carats. A ratti is a traditional weight, commonly taken as about 0.91 carat. Confirm in carats |
| Match | Report number, date and measurements match the stone you receive |

## Where the stories come from

| Story | What is told |
|---|---|
| Gems from a demon's body | The Garuda Purana tells that the gems arose from the body of a demon (Bala) who offered himself to the gods. Retellings differ on which part became which gem |
| The churning of the ocean | The Puranas tell of the Samudra Manthan, from which came the Moon, Lakshmi, the nectar and the Kaustubha jewel of Vishnu, often described as a ruby |
| Rahu and Ketu | The demon Svarbhanu drank the nectar and was severed by Vishnu. His head is Rahu and his body is Ketu |
| Testing gems | The Garuda Purana, the Agni Purana and Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita have sections on the qualities and faults of gems |
| The Navaratna | Nine gems for the nine planets, worn together in one setting or one by one |

| Look at | Why |
|---|---|
| The ascendant (lagna) | It sets which planets are friendly to your chart. The sun sign alone is not enough |
| The planet's place in your chart | Its sign, house and strength show whether it needs support |

| A friendly planet | A gem is held to strengthen its planet, so a difficult planet is usually not strengthened |

## Which finish to choose

| Finish | What differs | A good choice for |
|---|---|---|
| Essential | A good natural stone, smaller, with colour and cleanness that are pleasing and honest | A first stone, or a short period |
| Select | Richer colour, cleaner to the eye, origin stated | A sub-period of a year or more |
| Rare | The finest we offer: vivid colour, very clean, larger, with an independent lab report | Wearing for many years, as a keepsake |

All three finishes are natural, honestly described and backed by the AstroHQ guarantee. They differ in look and finish, not in what the tradition asks of a stone. Many people choose by how long they plan to keep and wear it. Your astrologer or a guide can tell you which planet's period is running, which is a different matter from the finish.

## Wearing and care

| Topic | Note |
|---|---|
| Wearing | Traditions differ on finger, metal and day. Ask [your astrologer](/consult) or family tradition |
| Ruby, sapphire | Hard and durable. Wash with mild soap and water |
| Emerald | Often oil or resin filled. Avoid ultrasonic cleaners, steam, hot water and solvents |
| Pearl, coral | Soft. Keep away from perfume, acid and rubbing. Wipe with a soft cloth |
