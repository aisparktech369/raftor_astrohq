## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Natural zircon, and zirconia (cubic zirconia) |
| Mineral | Zircon is zirconium silicate. Cubic zirconia is zirconium dioxide, made in laboratories |
| Hardness | Zircon 6.5 to 7.5. Cubic zirconia 8 to 8.5 |
| Traditional association | Used in practice as lower-cost alternatives to diamond |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Where it comes from

| Origin | What to know |
|---|---|
| Zircon: Sri Lanka, Cambodia, Myanmar | Long-standing sources |
| Zircon: Thailand, Tanzania, Australia | Further sources |
| Zirconia | No origin. It is made in laboratories, first at scale in the 1970s |

## Natural, treated, or made

| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| Zircon, natural | Colourless zircon is often heated to be colourless or blue. Must be stated |
| Zirconia, lab-made | Not a natural stone. It must be sold as cubic zirconia |
| Mixed up in the trade | Zirconia is sometimes sold as zircon. The report separates them |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the tradition says | Jyotish practice uses colourless zircon, and sometimes zirconia, as a lower-cost alternative to diamond. They are different stones |
| Texts | The Garuda Purana and Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita describe qualities and faults of gems |
| Status of the claim | The planetary link is a matter of religious tradition. It is not a scientific property of the stone or a promise of any result |

## What to ask for

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| An independent laboratory report | States zircon (natural) or cubic zirconia (lab-made) |
| Treatment in words | Heated or not |
| Weight | Zirconia is heavier than zircon for the same size |

<details><summary>Is cubic zirconia a real gem?</summary><p>It is a real material but not a natural stone. It should be sold as cubic zirconia.</p></details>
