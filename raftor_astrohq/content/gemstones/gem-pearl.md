## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Pearl, Moti |
| Mineral | Calcium carbonate (nacre) formed in a mollusc. An organic gem |
| Hardness | 2.5 to 4.5 on the Mohs scale. Soft |
| Traditional association | The Moon (Chandra) |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Where it comes from

| Origin | What to know |
|---|---|
| Japan, China | Akoya pearls, small, round and bright. China is the main freshwater source |
| Australia, Indonesia, Philippines | South Sea pearls, large, white to gold |
| French Polynesia | Tahitian pearls, dark with green and purple overtones |
| India | Cultured and freshwater work. Gulf of Mannar is a historic natural source |
| Persian Gulf | The historic source of natural pearls. Natural pearls are now rare |

## Natural, treated, or made

| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| Natural | Formed without human help. Rare and costly. Needs a laboratory report |
| Cultured | A nucleus is placed in the mollusc by people. A real pearl, sold as cultured |
| Treated | Bleached, dyed, coated or irradiated for colour. Must be stated |
| Imitation | Glass or plastic beads coated to look like pearls |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the tradition says | Jyotish practice links pearl with the Moon, and it is one of the nine gems of the Navaratna |
| Texts | The Garuda Purana and Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita describe qualities and faults of gems |
| Status of the claim | The planetary link is a matter of religious tradition. It is not a scientific property of the stone or a promise of any result |

## What to ask for

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| Natural or cultured, in words | Only a laboratory can tell with X-ray |
| Treatment | Bleached, dyed or coated |
| Type and size | Akoya, South Sea, Tahitian or freshwater, in millimetres |
| Care | Keep away from perfume, acid and rubbing. Wipe with a soft cloth |
