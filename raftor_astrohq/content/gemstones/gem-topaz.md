## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Topaz, blue topaz and white topaz |
| Mineral | Topaz (aluminium fluorosilicate) |
| Hardness | 8 on the Mohs scale, with perfect cleavage |
| Traditional association | Used in practice as lower-cost alternatives. Blue topaz for blue sapphire, white topaz for diamond |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Where it comes from

| Origin | What to know |
|---|---|
| Brazil | The main source of topaz |
| Sri Lanka, Nigeria | Colourless and pale material |
| Russia, Pakistan | Further sources |
| United States | Texas is known for blue topaz |

## Natural, treated, or made

| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| White topaz | Natural colourless topaz, usually only cut and polished |
| Blue topaz, treated | Colourless topaz irradiated and heated to give blue. Standard in the market. Must be stated |
| Natural blue topaz | Rare and pale |
| Look-alikes | Glass and cubic zirconia |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the tradition says | Jyotish practice uses blue topaz as a lower-cost alternative to blue sapphire and white topaz as one to diamond. They are different stones |
| Texts | The Garuda Purana and Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita describe qualities and faults of gems |
| Status of the claim | The planetary link is a matter of religious tradition. It is not a scientific property of the stone or a promise of any result |

## What to ask for

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| An independent laboratory report | States topaz and any treatment |
| Blue topaz treatment | Ask whether it is treated and that it comes from a reputable producer |
| Care | Perfect cleavage. Avoid knocks and sudden heat |

## Questions people ask

<details><summary>Is blue topaz the same as Neelam?</summary><p>No. Neelam is blue sapphire. Blue topaz is a different, much cheaper mineral.</p></details>

<details><summary>Is blue topaz natural?</summary><p>The stone is. Its blue colour is almost always made by treatment.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does it guarantee the traditional benefit people talk about?</summary><p>No. Traditional associations are matters of belief, not guaranteed outcomes.</p></details>
