## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Topaz, blue topaz and white topaz |
| Mineral | Topaz (aluminium fluorosilicate) |
| Hardness | 8 on the Mohs scale, with perfect cleavage |
| Traditional association | Used in practice as lower-cost alternatives. Blue topaz for blue sapphire, white topaz for diamond |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Where it comes from

| Origin | What to know |
|---|---|
| Brazil | The main source of topaz |
| Sri Lanka, Nigeria | Colourless and pale material |
| Russia, Pakistan | Further sources |
| United States | Texas is known for blue topaz |

## Natural, treated, or made

| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| White topaz | Natural colourless topaz, usually only cut and polished |
| Blue topaz, treated | Colourless topaz irradiated and heated to give blue. Standard in the market. Must be stated |
| Natural blue topaz | Rare and pale |
| Look-alikes | Glass and cubic zirconia |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the tradition says | Jyotish practice uses blue topaz as a lower-cost alternative to blue sapphire and white topaz as one to diamond. They are different stones |
| Texts | The Garuda Purana and Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita describe qualities and faults of gems |
| Status of the claim | The planetary link is a matter of religious tradition. It is not a scientific property of the stone or a promise of any result |

## Mythology and lore

| Story | What is told |
|---|---|
| Lore | No classical story is attached to topaz. The stories belong to Shani and Shukra, the planets it stands in for |
| Shani, son of Surya and Chhaya | Shani is the son of the Sun and his shadow-wife Chhaya, and brother of Yama. One tale (Brahma Vaivarta Purana) has his glance fall on the infant Ganesha. He is the slow planet and the giver of results of past actions |
| Shukracharya, teacher of the asuras | Shukracharya guided the asuras and held the Sanjivani vidya that revives the dead. In the story of Vamana at King Bali's sacrifice, he lost an eye to a blade of kusha grass |

## How astrologers decide

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| When astrologers consider it | When the planet is judged friendly to your birth chart (by the ascendant) and weak or badly placed, or when its major period is running. It is decided from the chart, not from the sun sign alone |
| The rule behind it | A gem is held to strengthen its planet, whatever the planet signifies for you. A planet that is difficult for your chart is usually not strengthened |

## Effectiveness: what to expect

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the tradition says | No classical themes are attached to this stone. Ask [your astrologer](/consult) what, if anything, it is advised for |
| How long | No fixed period in the cited texts. Practitioners commonly speak of watching for several weeks to a few months |
| The evidence | There is no scientific evidence that a gemstone changes events. Reported effects are personal and cannot be promised |
| Quality | The gem texts hold that a natural, clean stone of good colour, free of cracks and spots, is the one worth wearing. A lab-made, dyed or glass-filled stone is not the stone it is sold as |
| Weight | Set by your astrologer from the chart. As a buyer, compare the price per carat |
| A substitute | Held to act milder than the main gem. Practitioners differ on how much |
| If unsure | Stop wearing it if you feel uneasy and ask your astrologer. Do not change medical care or major decisions on the strength of a gem |

The stories and rules above are traditions, told as they are told. They are not claims of results.

## Questions people ask

<details><summary>Is blue topaz the same as Neelam?</summary><p>No. Neelam is blue sapphire. Blue topaz is a different, much cheaper mineral.</p></details>

<details><summary>Is blue topaz natural?</summary><p>The stone is. Its blue colour is almost always made by treatment.</p></details>
