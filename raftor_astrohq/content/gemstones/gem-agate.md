## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Agate (Hakik) and Sulemani hakik |
| Mineral | Chalcedony, a fine-grained quartz (silicon dioxide) |
| Hardness | 6.5 to 7 on the Mohs scale |
| Traditional association | Uses in practice vary. Ask your astrologer |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Where it comes from

| Origin | What to know |
|---|---|
| India (Gujarat) | A long-standing home of agate cutting and beadmaking |
| Brazil, Uruguay | The largest sources of agate |
| Madagascar | A modern source |
| Yemen | The old trade origin of the name Hakik (Arabic aqiq) |

## Natural, treated, or made

| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| Natural colour | Browns, greys and whites as they form |
| Dyed | Black, blue, green and red agate is usually dyed. Normal if stated |
| Sulemani hakik | Black with white or grey bands. Usually dyed |
| Look-alikes | Dyed glass, resin and plastic |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the tradition says | Agate is not one of the nine Navaratna. Its use in practice varies by family and astrologer |
| Texts | The Garuda Purana and Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita describe qualities and faults of gems |
| Status of the claim | The planetary link is a matter of religious tradition. It is not a scientific property of the stone or a promise of any result |

## What to ask for

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| An independent laboratory report | States chalcedony (agate) and any dye |
| Dye in words | "Dyed black agate" is honest and common |
| Bands | Natural bands follow the stone's shape |
