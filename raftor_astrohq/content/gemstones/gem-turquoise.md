## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Turquoise, Firoza |
| Mineral | Hydrated copper aluminium phosphate |
| Hardness | 5 to 6 on the Mohs scale. Porous |
| Traditional association | Uses in practice vary. Some link it with Jupiter or Venus |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Where it comes from

| Origin | What to know |
|---|---|
| Iran (Neyshabur) | The historic benchmark, a clear sky blue |
| United States | Arizona and Nevada, with Sleeping Beauty ceasing to produce in 2012 |
| China (Hubei) | The largest modern source |
| Tibet, Egypt (Sinai), Mexico | Further sources |

## Natural, treated, or made

| Kind | What it means |
|---|---|
| Natural, untreated | Rare and costly. Hard and dense |
| Stabilised | Resin is added to harden it. Common. Must be stated |
| Reconstituted | Powder pressed with resin. Not natural turquoise |
| Dyed or imitation | Dyed howlite, glass and plastic |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the tradition says | Turquoise is not one of the nine Navaratna. Its use in practice varies. Ask your astrologer |
| Texts | The Garuda Purana and Varahamihira's Brihat Samhita describe qualities and faults of gems |
| Status of the claim | The planetary link is a matter of religious tradition. It is not a scientific property of the stone or a promise of any result |

## What to ask for

| Ask | Why |
|---|---|
| An independent laboratory report | States natural turquoise and any stabilisation |
| Treatment in words | Untreated, stabilised, reconstituted |
| Care | Keep from oils, cosmetics, sweat and soaking |

<details><summary>Is howlite turquoise?</summary><p>No. Dyed howlite is a different mineral sold as turquoise.</p></details>
