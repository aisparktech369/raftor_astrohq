## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Name | Ganesh Mukhi, as the bead is described in the market |
| Classification | Kept distinct from the numbered mukhi system |
| Devotional association | Lord Ganesha |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| In devotion | Lord Ganesha is widely revered at the beginning of undertakings and associated with wisdom and the removal of obstacles. |
| Ganesh Mukhi | A bead described as Ganesh Mukhi draws on that devotional symbolism. |

## Mantra

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Mantra in use | *Om Gam Ganapataye Namah* (ॐ गं गणपतये नमः) is a widely used Ganesha devotional mantra, commonly understood as an invocation or salutation to Lord Ganapati. |
| In the text | It is a Ganesha mantra, not a mantra prescribed for a Ganesh Mukhi bead by the *Rudrakshajabala Upanishad*. |
| Choosing a mantra | Traditions may differ in pronunciation, ritual use and initiation requirements. |

## Mythology and lore

| Story | What is told |
|---|---|
| Origin of the bead | The Rudrakshajabala Upanishad tells that Rudra (Shiva) kept his eyes closed for a thousand divine years, and the tears that fell when he opened them became the Rudraksha. The name means the eyes of Rudra |
| Ganesha | The Puranas tell that Parvati made Ganesha, and Shiva gave him an elephant's head. He is worshipped first, at every beginning |

## Astrology at a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Planet in later practice | Ketu, in some lists. The Upanishad names deities, not planets. This link comes from later practice |
| Day | Monday, the day of Shiva, in common practice |
| Mantra | Om Namah Shivaya is the common mantra. See the Mantra table above |
| Who considers it | Mostly devotees of Shiva. Astrologers who link it with a planet judge that planet from the birth chart |
| Note | Practices differ by lineage. Follow your family or astrologer's practice |

## When to start and what to expect

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| When to start | Monday, Mahashivaratri, the month of Shravan or Pradosh are common choices |
| Before wearing | Wash it, offer flowers and chant Om Namah Shivaya 108 times. This is practice and differs by lineage |
| What to expect | Rudraksha is worn as a devotional aid. No result can be promised, and there is no scientific evidence of a physical effect |
| How long | No fixed period in the cited text. Practitioners speak of steady use over weeks and months |
| If unsure | Ask your guide or astrologer. Do not change medical care or major decisions on the strength of a bead |

## Questions people ask

<details><summary>Is Ganesh Mukhi the same as 8 Mukhi?</summary><p>No. Eight Mukhi refers to eight natural faces; Ganesh Mukhi is described through a distinctive form.</p></details>

<details><summary>Does it remove all obstacles?</summary><p>That is devotional symbolism, not a guaranteed outcome.</p></details>

<details><summary>Is it listed separately in the Rudraksha Jabala Upanishad?</summary><p>No. The text's numbered list does not separately describe this modern market category.</p></details>
