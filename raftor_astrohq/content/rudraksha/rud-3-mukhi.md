## At a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Mukhi | Three natural faces or divisions. |
| Traditional association | The three sacred fires; Agni. |
| Not promised | Wealth, health, or a guaranteed result |

## Meaning in the tradition

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What the text says | The Upanishad says the three-faced bead represents the three sacred fires and that Agni is pleased by its wearer. |
| Later explanations | Later explanations sometimes develop this into themes of purification, renewal and transformation. |
| Status of the claim | The Upanishadic association is a statement of religious tradition. It is not a scientific property of the bead or a guarantee that a wearer will experience a particular result. |

## Mantra

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Separate beej mantra | The cited Upanishad does not give a separate three-mukhi beej mantra. |
| General devotional mantra | Om Namah Shivaya (ॐ नमः शिवाय) may be used as a general Shaiva devotional mantra, not as a formula specific to this bead. |

## Mythology and lore

| Story | What is told |
|---|---|
| Origin of the bead | The Rudrakshajabala Upanishad tells that Rudra (Shiva) kept his eyes closed for a thousand divine years, and the tears that fell when he opened them became the Rudraksha. The name means the eyes of Rudra |
| Agni, mouth of the gods | Agni carries the offerings of the sacrifice to the gods. The Rigveda opens with a hymn to him. The three sacred fires are kept in the household rites |

## Astrology at a glance

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| Planet in later practice | Mars (Mangala). The Upanishad names deities, not planets. This link comes from later practice |
| Day | Monday, the day of Shiva, in common practice |
| Mantra | Om Namah Shivaya is the common mantra. See the Mantra table above |
| Who considers it | Mostly devotees of Shiva. Astrologers who link it with a planet judge that planet from the birth chart |
| Note | Practices differ by lineage. Follow your family or astrologer's practice |

## When to start and what to expect

| Topic | Detail |
|---|---|
| When to start | Monday, Mahashivaratri, the month of Shravan or Pradosh are common choices |
| Before wearing | Wash it, offer flowers and chant Om Namah Shivaya 108 times. This is practice and differs by lineage |
| What to expect | Rudraksha is worn as a devotional aid. No result can be promised, and there is no scientific evidence of a physical effect |
| How long | No fixed period in the cited text. Practitioners speak of steady use over weeks and months |
| If unsure | [Ask a guide or astrologer](/consult). Do not change medical care or major decisions on the strength of a bead |
